I
The Framing

Six skills, one fused ability.

The gap — hear note → search 10 notes → find it — is six skills in the app, not one. Scale degrees tell you which note you want. Note finding and shape memory tell you where it lives. CAGED ties chord shapes to chord tones across the neck. Chord-change hearing and intervals sharpen relative pitch. Improvisation applies chord-tone targeting on a backing track. Hero solos and song vocabulary (below, outside the app) are optional depth and repertoire work.

Every note is measured against the tonic, not the last note you played. The tonic is always there — droning under the music — and your ear is already measuring against it instinctively. You're not learning a new sense; you're learning to consciously notice what your ear has been doing all along.

App curriculum (80 levels)

The Tonic app implements this curriculum in lib/curriculum/levels.ts. Levels advance by mastery (sessions + accuracy), not calendar weeks. [F] = concept explainer first; [P] = practice only.

Track entry in the app: A, B, C from day one. D and E unlock after A·11 (full major diatonic by ear). F unlocks after A·11 and D·2 (vi chord by ear). A·12 (minor tonic) also requires C·2 (open A minor shape). C·7 requires A·4 (major 3rd by ear).

Completion: each level needs ≥3 sessions, ≥12 graded cards at ≥90% weighted accuracy (hints count half). Explainer-only levels A·1 and A·12 complete after the concept is seen.

V
Track A — The Spine

Scale Degrees

Each scale degree has a fixed character — stable, tense, sweet, pulling, restless, home. Learn the seven sounds once and they apply in every key forever. This is the layer that makes "hear it → play it" possible.

App end state · A·22 Hear any in-key note in common keys and name its degree. Minor phase (A·12+) needs C·2 (open A minor shape). Full level list in Section IV.
A·1–A·5 · Major foundation
Tonic → root → 5th → 3rd → stable consolidation.

Drone in C major. Each level: sing the degree, feel its character, find it on the neck. A·1 and A·12 are explainer-only (complete when seen).

  • A·1 Tonic — lock the key center.
  • A·2–A·4 Root, 5th, major 3rd (C·7 later needs A·4).
  • A·5 Practice — stable tones together.
A·6–A·11 · Complete major scale
7th, 4th, 2nd, 6th, then full diatonic.

Tense tones as motion away from chord tones. A·11 unlocks tracks D and E.

A·12–A·18 · Minor + cross-mode
A minor tonic (needs C·2), b3, b7, b6, full minor, consolidation.

Use the open A minor shape from Track C while droning Am. Pentatonic “escape” work lives in Track F once D·2 is done.

A·19–A·22 · Cross-key
G/D major, all major keys, E/D minor, all keys and modes.

Same degree characters, new key centers — the app rotates keys; your ear carries the function.

Track B — Foundation

Note Finding

Spatial recall in the app (flashcards + SRS). Separate from ear training; both reinforce each other. Pedagogy: one string at a time, then note-class anywhere on the neck, then sharps/flats and speed.

App end state · B·18 Chromatic recall across the full neck under time pressure. Maintenance stays in session reviews after completion.
B·1–B·7 · String by string
Low E → A (alone) → E+A mix → D → G → B → high E.
  • B·1 Low E — all naturals including open E.
  • B·2 A string only (not cross-string yet).
  • B·3 E + A mix — first cross-string recall.
  • B·4–B·7 D, G, B (2nd), high E strings.
B·8–B·14 · Note class (all strings)
Find every C, G, D, A, E, F, B — then mixed naturals.

Labels use note names, not “A string” (that was B·2). Progressive cross-string mode in the app.

B·15–B·18 · Chromatic & speed
Sharps, flats, mixed chromatic, timed full neck.

Optional offline: chromatic drills on each string still help warm up before cards.

VII
Track C — The Bridge

Fretboard Map & CAGED

Knowing what note you want (Track A) is useless without a physical map of where it lives. This track builds that map: starting with one minor scale shape, then pentatonic boxes, then connecting them through CAGED — the system that ties chord shapes to chord tones across the entire neck.

App end state · C·14 Connect pentatonic boxes and CAGED regions — chord tones in E, A, and D shapes, moving between positions. C·2 gates A·12; C·7 needs A·4.

CAGED, properly explained.

The cleanest way in is something you already do: barre chords.

When you play an F barre at the 1st fret, your hand makes an E-shape — same fingering as an open E chord, with a barre instead of the nut. When you play a B barre at the 2nd fret, your hand makes an A-shape. You're already using two of the five CAGED shapes whenever you barre.

The five shapes correspond to the five open chords: C, A, G, E, D. Any chord can be played in all five shapes somewhere on the neck. A G chord:

  • G in G-shape · open position (the standard cowboy G)
  • G in E-shape · 3rd fret barre
  • G in D-shape · 7th fret area
  • G in C-shape · 10th fret area
  • G in A-shape · 12th fret area

Slide up the neck through those five shapes in order — G → E → D → C → A — and you've played a G chord five different ways across the entire fretboard. The shapes always appear in that order. C-A-G-E-D loops endlessly.

The soloing payoff: each shape contains the chord's root, 3rd, and 5th in predictable spots. In the E-shape barre, the root is on strings 6, 4, and 1. The 3rd is on string 3. The 5th is on strings 5 and 2. Once you know that pattern for one shape, you can find chord tones for any chord you're playing in that shape — anywhere on the neck.

CAGED isn't a separate scale system. It's a way of organizing chord tones across the neck using shapes you mostly already know.

C·1–C·4 · Open chords & movable roots
Open C, open A minor (gates A·12), movable major/minor roots.

Get C·2 early so A·12 can unlock. Physical shape memory before pentatonic boxes.

C·5–C·7 · Pentatonic boxes
Box 1 → Box 2 → chord tones inside the boxes.

Identify roots, b3, 5, b7 inside each box — fuse with Track A degrees. C·7 requires hearing the major 3rd (A·4).

C·8–C·13 · CAGED chord tones
E-shape → A-shape → D-shape (roots, 3rds, 5ths in each).

Drill: play the chord, then only roots, only 3rds, only 5ths. Minor = flattened 3rd in the same shape.

C·14 · Connect positions
Move between boxes and CAGED regions without breaking phrasing.

C and G CAGED shapes can come later in personal practice; the app focuses on E, A, D for soloing maps.

Intervals

Relative pitch: hear the distance between two notes. Unlocks after A·11. Order is consonance-first (unison, perfect 5th, major 3rd, …) — not “count semitones from the root” first.

App end state · E·17 Identify common intervals in isolation and in short melodic fragments. Supports transcription and hero-solo analysis (Section XI).
E·1–E·10 · Consonant & core intervals
Unison through octave — build the palette.

Each level adds one interval quality; consolidation levels mix the set learned so far. See Section IV for exact names.

Tritone, compound intervals, real-world drills.

Improvisation

Play chord-tone targets over a backing track — the app grades whether you hit the requested degree. Unlocks after A·11 and D·2 (vi by ear).

App end state · F·4 Move chord tones (1, 3, 5) when harmony changes — pentatonic “escape” as passing motion, not a pool of equal notes.
F·1–F·4
Root → triad tones → motion across changes.
  • F·1 Target roots over a static vamp.
  • F·2–F·3 Add 3rds and 5ths — restrict to chord tones first.
  • F·4 Chord tones that shift when the progression moves.

Record yourself in personal practice; the app session may include freeplay after structured cards.

XIII
Recurring Micro-Practice

Transposition Practice

Not a track — a recurring 5-minute warmup or cooldown. The most direct test of whether scale-degree thinking is actually working in your hands. Once you can transpose freely, you've internalized the thinking.

End State · Wk 16Take any short melody you know and play it in any key on demand, by hearing the intervals/degrees rather than calculating fret math.
i.
Simple Melodies
Nursery rhymes, well-known riffs (Happy Birthday, Twinkle Twinkle, Smoke on the Water). Transpose to one new key.
ii.
Any Key on Demand
Same melodies, but now transpose to any key without warning.
iii.
Song Phrases
Transpose phrases from songs in Section XII (song vocabulary).
iv.
Hero Solo
The full hero-solo transposition exercise — multiple keys, scale-degree thinking only.

Start at Stage I in Wk 5. Move up a stage roughly every 3–4 weeks. 5 min/day as a warmup or cooldown — never the centerpiece of a session.

XVI
Honest Notes

Priorities & Permissions

If a week feels overwhelming

In the app: protect A and B. Personal work drops first.

Skip song vocabulary or hero solo weeks before skipping Tonic sessions. Within the app, if you're overloaded, the builder still prioritizes current levels — but don't let weeks pass with zero A/B cards.

Tracks A (degrees) and B (note finding) are the spine. C is the bridge. D, E, F unlock when ready — pausing personal repertoire is fine; pausing A·progress is not.

A note on phrasing
You're handling technique in parallel — but the hero solo IS phrasing work.

Three notes with great phrasing sound like Hazel. Fifty correct notes with no phrasing sound like an exercise. The theory tells you which notes; phrasing makes them mean something. Don't let one lap the other.

A note on the ear
You have ten years of instincts. Trust them when they push back.

If a week's drill feels wrong in your hands, that's data. Adjust the plan, not your ear.

A note on the timeline
Levels beat weeks.

If mastery gates aren't met, stay on the level — the app won't advance you early. Section XV weeks are optional pacing hints only.
